# Summary

Guidance from the research-ethics committee system — the medical research-ethics committees and the
National Research Ethics Committee — in the form of a checklist for research projects that develop,
use or test AI-based health technology. It supports the writing, management and assessment of such
projects, and the weight of each question rises with the model's maturity, complexity and risk [1].

# Key provisions

- Training material: describe what the model is trained on, how the data were collected (with consent where relevant), and how they are documented and quality-assured.
- Bias: account for skews in training data, and test and monitor the model's output for bias during development and in operation.
- Risks: describe how the model can fail, how errors are identified, reported and handled, and how misuse and unintended consequences are managed.
- Explainability: those affected by the model's output should be able to understand what it rests on and its limits, at a meaningful level.
- Responsibility: state who holds final responsibility for output, how decisions are documented, and which governance structures handle harm.
