# Summary

A section of the patient-safety authority's guidance page on digital health-professional
treatment, setting out who carries responsibility when AI is used in diagnosis, assessment of
patient data or planning of treatment, and what the authority examines when AI-assisted
treatment proves to be wrong [1].

# Key provisions

- Output from an AI system used in treatment is always to be treated as decision support; it does not replace the health professional's own professional responsibility.
- The responsible professional must make an independent assessment of whether an AI recommendation is relevant and sound in the situation, so there must be relevant human professional control of AI recommendations.
- Responsibility may be shared: the treating professional, the treatment site or its management (procedures, training, supervision, quality assurance), and the developer or supplier (design faults, insufficient testing).
- When AI-assisted treatment proves wrong, the authority looks at intended use, human control, the professional assessment, the site's training and quality assurance, the supplier's information on limitations, and faults in the system.
- Questions about the AI product itself and medical-device rules belong to Lægemiddelstyrelsen.
