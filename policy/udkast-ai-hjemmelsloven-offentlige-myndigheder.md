# Summary

A draft act giving all public authorities and bodies a general legal basis to process personal data
when they develop and use AI systems, decision support included. It applies across the public
sector; its remarks use clinical examples, such as an AI system suggesting which patients a doctor
should see first or proposing a course of treatment to health staff [1][2]. The consultation note
records that the act does not regulate AI in health separately and leaves the health legislation,
the AI Act and the Medical Device Regulation unchanged [3].

# Key provisions

- Public authorities and bodies may develop and use AI systems, decision support included, on the personal data they already process for their tasks.
- *Decision support* is defined as AI-generated suggestions that a person weighs in a professional assessment, without the system making the decision; the remarks read it to include the health sector's *beslutningsstøtte ved patientbehandling*.
- Each minister may set specific safeguards for sensitive data within their own area, and may restrict certain data-subject rights.
- The act would enter into force the day after its publication.

# Notes

The consultation drew 41 responses by the deadline. Danske Regioner and Dansk Selskab for Almen
Medicin are among those whose comments on clinical AI and patient rights are summarised under a
heading of their own; the ministry answers that sector-specific rules continue to apply and that the
interplay will be taken up in coming guidance [3]. Whether the bill has been
introduced in the Folketing is not yet recorded in this vault: ft.dk could not be read on 2026-11-25.
